= Who owns the port — domain, port, and edge, told apart
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

[NOTE]
====
*Status:* DESIGN (2026-06-22). A companion to
link:frontier-playability-model.adoc[the frontier-playability model]. That doc names the three axes
(playability / edge / direction) and the two edge species. This one answers the question that keeps
recurring when extracting a `<target>-edge`: *what tells an edge apart from a domain, and who owns the
port between them?* It exists because we twice mis-modelled it — proposing a `ssh-to-age-port` and a
`ssh-to-age-core` for a thing that has neither.
====

== The three roles in one sentence

The *domain* is what is enclosed; the *edge* is its face toward an outside; the *port* is the membrane
between them — *and the port is owned by the consumer that calls it, never by the edge.*

Each role has exactly one verb. Keeping the verbs straight is the whole model:

[cols="1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Role | Verb | What it is / owns

| *domain* (`-core`)
| *CALLS*
| Pure logic that reasons over the model and never leaves the system (remove every external system, it
  still compiles and computes). Owns its model types; *declares + calls* the ports it needs (`@Reference`).

| *port* (`-contract`)
| *DECLARES*
| The shared interface of call + the pure gateway records. A *passive type*, exported and wired
  bundle-to-bundle so its consumers inside the OSGi world share one class. It is NOT resolved and carries
  no _resolution/service_ capability — no `@Component`. (It does carry one passive *discovery marker*,
  `type=contract` — see <<the-boot-face,the boot face>> — which the resolver ignores, being a namespace
  with no `Require` side.) It is the *address*, not the door. Owned by the consumer (lives in its
  `-contract` module), never named after the edge.

| *edge* (`<target>-edge`)
| *PROVIDES*
| The adapter whose reason to exist is *contact* with one external target (remove the target, nothing is
  left). *Implements* a port and *provides the capability* that makes that port reachable — it is the
  door SCR resolves. Owns its low-level contact types; exposes NO port of its own.
|===

== Who is resolved — the port is the address, the edge is the door

This is the pivot the whole model turns on, and the one we kept fumbling:

[quote]
____
The *port is not resolved* — it only provides the interface of call (the shared type). What is resolved
is the wiring between a *consumer that requires* and an *edge that provides*, expressed *through* that
type. The port is the address; the edge is the door.
____

So a port that is merely exported (`Export-Package`) gives *type resolution only* — the contract's class,
nothing about who implements it (the `system-export = resolution-only` invariant). Type-sharing alone
finds no door: a consumer holding just the package would have to `new EdgeImpl()`, re-coupling to the
edge (anti-DIP). The door is found because the *edge publishes a capability* keyed on the port type, and
the consumer requires it.

Read it as: the consumer (`①`) and the edge (`②`) both point AT the port — the port points at nothing,
which is why it is pure and passive. The actual resolution (`③`) runs consumer↔edge, *keyed on the port
type* but not flowing through the port as an actor. The edge carries the behaviour (the capability); the
port carries only the type. *That* is what tells port from edge — not "one is pure and the other does
I/O", but "the port is the type, the edge is the capability that makes the type reachable".

=== Two worlds, two resolution regimes

Whether the edge must publish a capability *depends on the consumer's world*:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Consumer world | How the door is found

| *OSGi world* (e.g. ssh-to-age's consumer)
| The edge MUST publish a capability, else no bundle finds it without a coupling `new`. Two strengths:
  *(a) service* — `@Component` provides `osgi.service` keyed on the port type, `@Reference` binds it;
  *runtime, soft* (no edge → component just stays unsatisfied). *(b) resolution capability* —
  `Provide-/Require-Capability` on a namespace; *resolve-time, hard* (no door → the framework refuses to
  wire). For a *system boundary*, (b) is the better fail-mode: "no door ⟹ resolution fails" beats
  "silently inactive". Precedent: the unitrepo handler edge (`osgi.extender=unitrepo.handler` + a
  `unitrepo.type` namespace).

| *host world* (e.g. pulumi-edge, dbus-edge)
| NO capability needed *today*. The host is the flat assembler — it does
  `new PulumiInterventionLedgerWriter(...)` explicitly in `DoctorAssembly`. There is no registry to
  resolve against, so the edge provides nothing; it is simply instantiated by the wiring. This is why
  `pulumi-edge` never had to declare a capability.
|===

=== The world is DERIVED from the consumer, not a property of the edge

A correction worth stating loudly: an edge's world *follows the world of whoever calls it*. ssh-to-age
is the first external edge in the OSGi world for one reason — its consumer
(`DefaultManifestSynthesisService`) is a `@Component`. pulumi/dbus are host because their callers
(`DoctorAssembly`, the readiness gates) are host (non-playability is only the *secondary* reason their
impl could not move even if a caller did). So "which world?" is never answered by looking at the edge
alone; look at who requires it.

=== The registry is the single source of resolution (target) — vs the mixed present

The *target* state: a consumer always obtains an edge through the OSGi registry — never through a hand
written `if (host) new … else @Reference`. How the impl *enters* the registry follows playability
(`@Component` SCR when playable; a host-published `registerService` when not), but the *resolution* the
consumer sees is uniform: it requires the port type, the registry supplies the door.

*The boot trap (resolved — `refactor/osgi-boot-single-source`).* The `META-INF/bundles` embed topology
WAS hand-listed at ~8 sites with no single source — `OsgiRuntime` admitted it ("poms cannot call Java,
this is the authoritative registry during review"). It is now single-sourced by a *capability scan*: each
model bundle self-declares `Provide-Capability: io.seedmatic.rke2lab.embed; type=model` in its `bnd.bnd`,
and `OsgiRuntime.boot()` scans `META-INF/bundles`, reads each jar's manifest, and installs exactly the
carriers of that capability. The boot-stack jars (pax/scr/resolver) do not carry the marker, so they are
excluded *objectively* — not by a name filter, which would be a hand-list in disguise that slips at the
first rename. All the Java bundle-name literals (`bootingEmbedded("…jar")`, the builder's
`embeddedBundle(…)` verb, the tests' `embeddedBundle(…)`) are gone; adding an embedded bundle is now one
pom `artifactItem` + one capability line in its bnd, zero Java. The staging `artifactItem` and the
shade `<exclude>` remain the two pom faces of one declaration (the shade runs before the boot, so it
cannot be scanned), but that is the irreducible pom side — the Java hand-list is eliminated. This is the
same gesture as the registry above: *read the real artifact* (the capability the jar actually declares),
never a parallel hand-maintained list — `model-substrate-alignment` applied to the build. See
link:../osgi/osgi-boot-single-source-handoff.adoc[the OSGi boot single-source handoff].

[[the-boot-face]]
== The boot face — how each role loads (the seam, and why we keep losing it)

Ownership tells the roles apart at *design* time. But they also differ at *boot* time — in which
classloader each role's classes live — and that difference is the one we keep forgetting and then
rediscovering the hard way (a split class, a silent `ClassCastException` across the host seam). This
section pins it down so it stays pinned.

The host (`seed-outcluster` and the two CLIs) runs *flat, outside OSGi* on the JVM application classloader
(JCL). It boots an embedded Felix and speaks to it across ONE membrane — the seed-broker seam. It
exchanges services across it — e.g. it publishes `RunGate` / `OpaqueCellar` / `SeedEnvelope` and awaits the
`SeedBroker`, all `seed-broker-port` types. For that typed handoff to work (no reflection, no
`ClassCastException`), the flat host *and* the installed bundle must share *one copy* of the seam class.
There is exactly one mechanism for one shared copy: the *system bundle* exports the package
(`FRAMEWORK_SYSTEMPACKAGES_EXTRA`), so every bundle imports the host's class rather than loading its own.
That shared-export boundary is the *seam* — and today only `seed-broker-port` sits on it.

[IMPORTANT]
====
[NOTE]
====
*Done — the domains are de-seamed; `seed-broker-port` alone remains the seam.* This section used to
teach "every port is a seam". That law is *retired*. The
link:../osgi/seed-broker-spec.adoc[seed-broker design] § "One seam only" reframed it: once the host
speaks ONLY to the broker, the domain ports stop being inter-WORLD seams and become inter-DOMAIN APIs
(consumed inside OSGi, never host-side). That end-state is now realised on disk. The seven domain
boundary modules — `auth-contract`, `cluster-contract`, `doctor-contract`, `incus-contract`,
`manifests-contract`, `netplan-contract`, `systemd-contract` — are each `type=contract`: an installed
bundle, wired bundle-to-bundle inside the OSGi world, NOT system-exported. The ONLY `type=seam` left is
`seed-broker-port`.

*The concrete de-seaming mechanic (`bbox` was the first, 2026-07-11).* `bbox-port` (type=seam)
dissolved into `bbox-core` (type=model, installed): its records + the `BboxReconciler` interface +
the pure `BlueprintRowEnumerator`, wired bundle-to-bundle to the edge (which `@Component`-provides
the reconciler) and the `bbox-bdd` scion (which resolves + drives it), both in-container. No
system-export, no seam. Two shapes emerged: a domain whose port is *pure logic + records* folds
entirely into its `-core` (the bbox case); a domain that keeps a distinct boundary vocabulary keeps a
module for it, renamed `-contract` and re-typed `type=contract` (the seven above). Either way: rewire
importers off the seam, install the bundle, wire it bundle-to-bundle.

*Nuance the bbox case taught — a host import is not a reason to KEEP the seam.* The rule "shed when
zero host files import it" read backwards during the migration: `bbox` still had host importers (in
the DEFERRED `exec/seed-outcluster` — e.g. `BboxStage` resolving `BboxReconciler` host-side, and the
`doctor.contract` leak where `SystemdAdapterStage`/`ClusterReadinessStage` consult `ConsultingService`
directly). Those host imports ARE the leak the migration removes, not a licence to keep the seam:
the host must speak only gardening, so a host file naming a domain contract is a bug to delete, not a
constraint to honour. We de-seamed in the OSGi world regardless; the deferred host is rewritten to stop
importing the contract (it grafts the scion / consults the broker instead). The seam did not wait on
the leak — the leak waits on the host migration. `seed-broker-port` alone stays a genuine seam:
RunGate + OpaqueCellar + SeedEnvelope ARE published by the host from outside Felix — the one real
host↔OSGi membrane.
====

*The seam law (the load-bearing invariant, today).* A class of the *seam* (`seed-broker-port`) is loaded
by the *host JCL* and shared into the framework via a system export. A class of a *domain* (`-core`),
its *contract* (`-contract`), or an *edge* is loaded by its *bundle classloader* and never escapes to
the JCL. Equivalently, stated as the guard:

* `type=model` / `type=contract` / `type=edge` package ⟹ exported by *its bundle only*, **never** in
  `system.packages.extra`. Leaking it there gives two copies (flat + bundle) and splits the class.
* `type=seam` package (and genuinely non-OSGi third-party jars with no `Bundle-SymbolicName`) ⟹
  exported by the *system bundle*, shared with the flat host. This is deliberate, and the *only* thing
  that legitimately belongs there — today `seed-broker-port` alone.

A seam carries a `Bundle-SymbolicName` (it is a real jar), so "has a BSN ⟹ install it as a bundle"
is *too strong* — it would install the seam as its own bundle, give the host a second copy, and break
the very typed handoff the seam exists for. The discriminator is not "is it a bundle" but "which side of
the seam does it load on", and that is *declared*, not inferred. A `-contract` also carries a BSN and is
installed as a bundle — the correct disposition, since it never crosses to the host.
====

So the embed namespace `io.seedmatic.rke2lab.embed` carries the seam `type` alongside the ownership roles'
boot faces:

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| `type=` | Role | Boot face

| `model`
| domain (`-core`) with `@Component`
| Installed as a bundle (BundleCL). Its exported packages stay its own — never system-exported.

| `contract`
| domain port (`-contract`) — the boundary vocabulary a domain declares
| Installed as a bundle (BundleCL). Wired bundle-to-bundle to its consumers inside the OSGi world; its
  packages never leak to the system bundle. (Was `type=seam` before the domains de-seamed.)

| `edge`
| `<target>-edge` with `@Component`
| Installed as a bundle (BundleCL). Same: its packages never leak to the system bundle.

| `seam`
| `seed-broker-port` (the sole host↔OSGi membrane)
| NOT installed as a bundle. System-exported so the flat host shares one typed copy. The JCL side of
  the boundary.

| `fixture`
| test-only carrier (+ `suite`/`role`)
| Installed only by the test harness (`OutOfContainerFrameworkExtension`), never on a runtime classpath.
|===

The one question the `type=seam` value answers — the key phrase to keep:

[quote]
____
*Which side of the seam frontier does the port load on?* — the system bundle (JCL), shared with the flat
host, or its own bundle (BundleCL), kept inside the framework.
____

That is the whole meaning of the value, and it is *factual, not metaphorical*: in OSGi exactly one
classloader may *define* a given package; everyone else *imports* that one copy (one package = one
exporter = one class). So "which side does it load on" is just "which classloader defines this package —
the system bundle, or a specific bundle?". `type=seam` declares the answer is *the system bundle* (so the
flat host and the bundles share one typed copy); `type=model`/`type=contract`/`type=edge` declares the
answer is *that bundle alone* (and the system bundle must never also define it, or the two copies split
the class).

Why a *declared* `type=seam` and not an inferred "no `@Component` ⟹ seam": inference is negative and
fragile — the day the seam grew a component it would silently flip from seam to domain and the seam
would break with no warning. Declaring the seam positively, in the same namespace as the rest, is the
single-source-of-truth gesture applied here: the bundle *says* which side of the frontier it loads on,
the boot reads it, nothing guesses. The guard then fail-fasts (naming the package) if a
`type=model`/`type=contract`/`type=edge` package would ever reach `system.packages.extra`.

The boot machinery that reads this seam law — the pure `BootPlanner` that derives `system.packages.extra`
and runs the guard, told apart from the `FrameworkLauncher` that enacts it — is decomposed in
link:../osgi/osgi-boot-decomposition-spec.adoc[the OSGi boot decomposition]. Its pax/JCL volet sharpens
the same frontier from the build side: a bundle (BundleCL) that leaks onto the flat host classpath (JCL)
is a *wrong classpath*, so the boot stack is `optional` and staged by coordinate rather than left flat.

== The decisive test — domain vs edge

[quote]
____
Does this type *cross a boundary of the system*, or stay inside it reasoning over the model?
____

* Crosses → *edge*.
* Stays inside, computes → *domain* (or *port*, if it is the contract the domain calls).

The trap the playability axis dispels: the discriminator is *not* "does I/O / spawns a process". A
domain may read a file; `ProcessBuilder` and `java.nio` are playable. The discriminator is *reaching an
external target* — the skin of the system, not the act of I/O.

== The ownership rule (the load-bearing claim)

*A port belongs to the consumer that calls outward — the domain or the orchestration. The edge
IMPLEMENTS that port and PROVIDES the capability behind it; it never owns the port.* This is
hexagonal/DIP stated for our layout: the adapter depends on the contract, the contract never depends on
the adapter.

Consequences, both observed on the real reactor:

* There is *no `pulumi-port`*. The ports `pulumi-edge` implements — `SnapshotSource`,
  `InterventionLedgerWriter` — live in `doctor-contract`, because *doctor* is the consumer that needs to read
  state and write a ledger. Nobody ever created a port named after the edge.
* A pure point of contact with *no domain* (ssh-to-age: it reasons over nothing, it just converts by
  shelling a tool) gets *no `-core` and no port of its own* — only an `-edge`, plus a port owned by
  whoever calls it.

== Peer-to-peer mediation — the keystone is dissolved, not enthroned

[NOTE]
====
*Revision (2026-06-23) — this section reverses an earlier claim.* It used to read "the *keystone*: the
control-node is the one place that knows two facets at once, therefore the one place that can mediate;
mediation between facets is a property of the executable, never a domain." That thesis is *withdrawn*.
The mechanism below (a domain *contributes* its bridge as a fragment, the registry resolves it) shows
mediation is *distributed*, not central. The dbus spec's keystone section is superseded the same way.
====

Start from the same true premise — *knowledge runs strictly downward.* Each domain is pure in its
*upward ignorance*: it does not know which application embeds it (Pulumi does not know it provisions
RKE2; the doctor does not know it lives in a provisioning system; systemd does not know a doctor
auscultates it). The dependency graph enforces this — `seed-outcluster` depends on *every* domain; *no
domain depends on `seed-outcluster`*.

The old conclusion was that the control-node, being the only thing that sees two facets, *must* be the
mediator. But that confused two different controls under one word:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Control of… | Who holds it

| *meaning* — _which facets relate, and how_
| *Distributed, peer-to-peer.* A domain that intrinsically spans peers relates them *itself*, in a
  *contribution* it owns, reaching the peers *through their ports*. cluster-readiness presupposes the
  node's systemd convergence — so the *cluster* relates cluster↔systemd↔doctor, not the executable.
  The registry resolves the wiring; no center decides it.

| *time* — _when, how often, retry, preview, boot the framework_
| *The executable, and only it.* This is nobody's domain: something must start Felix and hold the
  readiness poll. The control-node keeps this — but it is *demoted from keystone to a bootstrapper +
  time-driver*. It carries no domain meaning; remove every line and no reasoning is left in it.
|===

So the arch has *no crown stone*. The least-knowledge rule is the whole point: *each component relates
exactly the facets it already knows, and the less it knows the better it does its one job.* A domain
spanning peers is not a leak as long as (a) it reaches them only through *ports* (never impls), (b) the
graph stays acyclic (the peer never learns about it back), and (c) the span is *essential* — a
real-world presupposition, not a convenience. Where no domain intrinsically spans two facets, that
mediation has no owner and *falls back* to the executable — subsidiarity, not centrality.

What used to be a *sequence the control-node decided* (produce a systemd fact → translate → consult the
doctor) becomes a *data dependency inside the contribution*: the cluster's mediator reads the systemd
node-fact itself and produces the `Observation`. Sequencing dissolves into wiring; only the temporal
poll remains in the executable. (The edge species this still produces — *sondes* that
observe→snapshot→fact→consult, versus *portes* that convert with no persisted fact — are catalogd in
link:frontier-playability-model.adoc[the frontier-playability model].)

[[the-contribution-role]]
== The fourth role — a contribution (a fragment that brings a bridge to a host)

The three ownership roles (domain / port / edge) describe *one* domain's parts. Peer-to-peer mediation
needs a fourth, *across* domains: a **contribution**. It is how a domain that spans peers presents its
bridge to a *host* that fans contributions out — without the host ever learning the domain.

[cols="1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Role | Verb | What it is / owns

| *contribution* (a *fragment* of a host)
| *CONTRIBUTES*
| A domain's bridge made present inside a host. It attaches to *one* host (`Fragment-Host`), carries a
  live SCR `@Component` *mediator* (produces a fact by composing other domains' ports/edges) and/or a
  *reasoner* the host fans out to (e.g. a `Specialist`), and brings its *own* `Import-Package` for the
  peers it spans. Owns no port. The coupling to its peers lives *here*, by definition — so the host's
  manifest names no domain and stays blind.
|===

Three facts make this work, and each is load-bearing:

* *A fragment carries live components.* SCR processes the `Service-Component` header of a bundle *and of
  its attached fragments* (DS spec 112.4.1); a fragment's `@Component` activates in the *host's* bundle
  context. So a mediator that `@References` other domains' ports lives in the contribution, not the host.
* *Resolve precedes activation, so the host adapts.* A fragment is attached at *resolve* time; by
  *activation* the host already knows its fragments. The host therefore stops hard-wiring its roster
  (`roster.add(new XSpecialist())`) and reads *whatever was contributed* (a dynamic `@Reference
  List<…>`, or the woven components). The host adapts to how it was contributed — it does not enumerate
  its contributors by name.
* *The fragment brings its own imports.* Sharing the host classloader, the contribution can reach the
  host's package-private internals (this is the *fragment-test model* — `*-core-test` fragments already
  do it — promoted to production), and it adds *its* `Import-Package` (the peer ports) to the host. The
  host stays decoupled; the bridge's coupling is quarantined in the fragment.

Two decoupled times live in one contribution without contradiction: the *mediator* face *measures*
(time 1, opens the door, produces the fact) and the *reasoner* face *reads* (time 2, never probes) —
the same "doctors do not open doors" split as link:../osgi/dbus-systemd-edge-spec.adoc[the dbus spec],
now *inside* a single contributed fragment rather than across the executable.

[[mediation-proof-obligation]]
[IMPORTANT]
====
*Proven (2026-06-23) — the mechanism holds, with conditions.* A `@Component` that lives in a FRAGMENT
is activated by Felix SCR (2.2.x / framework 7.0.5) in the HOST's context — reaching state `ACTIVE`,
references satisfied, no failure, read from SCR's own `ComponentConfigurationDTO`. The proof is
`FragmentContributedComponentTest` in `osgi/bench` (host `bench-fragment-host` + fragment
`bench-fragment-contribution`). Three non-obvious conditions the host must meet — they are *not*
automatic, so a contribution host (e.g. `doctor-core`) must declare them:

. The fragment carries the `@Component`; bnd emits its `OSGI-INF/*.xml` + `Service-Component` into the
  fragment jar.
. *The host* declares `Service-Component: OSGI-INF/*.xml` (wildcard) in its own bnd. SCR reads the
  header off the host bundle (`getHeaders()` does not merge fragment headers), but the wildcard
  resolves against the host's entries via `findEntries`, which spans attached fragments.
. *The host* declares `Require-Capability: osgi.extender;filter:="(osgi.extender=osgi.component)"` by
  hand — a host owning no `@Component` makes bnd emit no extender requirement, so SCR would not track
  it.

Observation caveat: the published service's *type* must have a single classloader. If two bundles both
define the service package, the two copies split the class and a lookup never matches. For the doctor
this does not arise — the observable type lives in `doctor-contract` (`type=contract`), an installed
bundle that is its sole exporter, wired bundle-to-bundle to its consumers inside the OSGi world; no
second copy exists.
====

[[vocabulary-sentences]]
== The roles as sentences — the vocabulary read aloud

Each role is one *sentence* built from the shared vocabulary: a subject, its one verb, and what it
sees. The *coupling signature* — how many ports the subject sees — is right there in the object of the
sentence, and it is what tells the roles apart. Capability is universal (every port is exported, visible
to any bundle that wires to it), but *use is minimal*: a role sees only the ports its sentence names.

[cols="2,5",options="header"]
|===
| Sentence | Ports it sees

| *A domain-core CALLS the ports it needs and reasons over its own model.*
| The ports it *calls* (several allowed — they are its declared needs). Sees no edge, no other core.

| *A port DECLARES a type and points at nothing.*
| None. It is passive — the address, not the door. Both sides point at it; it points nowhere.

| *An edge IMPLEMENTS one port and CONTACTS one external target.*
| Exactly *one* — the port it implements. An edge that sees two ports is a mediator in disguise.
  (Verified: `pulumi-edge` imports exactly one, `doctor-contract`.)

| *A mediator RELATES several ports and contacts no external target.*
| *Several* — the ports of the facets it bridges. A mediator that sees one relates nothing.

| *A contribution CONTRIBUTES a mediator and-or a reasoner to one host.*
| The peer ports its mediator bridges, brought as its *own* `Import-Package`. Attaches to one host
  (`Fragment-Host`); the host's manifest names no domain.
|===

....
flowchart LR
  domaincore["domain-core --- CALLS --- the ports it needs"]
  port["port --- DECLARES --- nothing"]
  edge["edge --- IMPLEMENTS one port --- CONTACTS one target"]
  mediator["mediator --- RELATES several ports --- no target"]
  contribution["contribution --- CONTRIBUTES to one host"]

....

The reading that settles edge-vs-mediator: it is *not* "edge does I/O, mediator does not" — both reach
through ports. It is the *count*: one port (a contact) versus several (a bridge). The verb and the
port-count together are the whole identity.

== Direction is a method, not a module, not two ports

A second recurring confusion: expecting an edge to split into an "in" port and an "out" port.

* *One edge = one target = one door.* It is not directional. The same `pulumi-edge` both reads (ingress)
  and writes (egress) — splitting it by direction would lie about it being one tool, and `ingress`
  collides with the k8s sense anyway.
* *Two ports does not mean in/out.* `pulumi-edge` implements two ports because the *domain* (doctor) has
  two distinct needs — read a snapshot, write a ledger — so they are named *by concern*
  (`SnapshotSource`, `InterventionLedgerWriter`), never `…In`/`…Out`.
* *Direction lives in the method name / prose.* `SnapshotSource.timeline()` is ingress;
  `LedgerWriter.up()` is egress. If a single contract one day both reads and writes, the edge stays one
  module with methods of both directions.

== Worked example B — ssh-to-age-edge (a point of contact, NO domain)

ssh-to-age reasons over nothing: it converts a key by shelling an external tool. There is no residual
computation if you remove the tool — so *no `-core`, and no port named after it*. The contract belongs to
the *consumer* that needs the age key.

Per the prerequisite design, the synthesis unit must not *fetch* the key mid-synthesis. The consumer is
`DefaultManifestSynthesisService` — the `@Component` (OSGi world, our lock): it runs a *pre-synthesis
pass* that calls the converter and *binds the age key into the profile-bearing context*, the same channel
that already carries `IncusIdentityMaterial` / `BootstrapIdentity`. That channel is
`ManifestSynthesisContext` (the request/ThreadLocal-scoped profile holder) — NOT `ManifestsUnitContext`,
which is the per-unit record (`chart`, `domainId`, `resolver`) and carries no profile. `doSynthesize`
then only reads the key from the context and builds the Secret:

== Worked example C — cluster (a domain that CONTRIBUTES a bridge to a host)

====

<<mediation-proof-obligation,the proof obligation>> before it is built.

cluster is the case the first two examples do not cover: a *sonde* whose readiness *presupposes another
domain's fact*. "The cluster is ready" means *kubeconfig published ∧ API ready ∧ controllers effective*
— and the first of those presupposes the node's *systemd convergence*. That presupposition is a
real-world coupling; the question is only where it lives. Peer-to-peer says: in the cluster's own
contribution, never in the executable.

It carries a vocabulary *about the cluster* the consumer reads but does not own (the readiness phases),
so unlike ssh-to-age it *does* get a port — symmetric with systemd, not with pulumi:

* *cluster-contract* — `ClusterReadinessProbe` (the contact seam the edge implements), purified to a pure
  `ClusterProbeRequest` record (no host `BootstrapConfig`); `ClusterReadinessPhase` (today it *squats*
  in `doctor-contract` — a latent debt, re-homed here); the phase↔`SchemaRef` enum (today loose literals in
  `ClusterSpecialist`). Owned by the cluster domain, read cross-domain by the doctor.
* *cluster-edge* — `@Component implements ClusterReadinessProbe`, kubectl over `ProcessBuilder` for the
  api-ready and controllers-effective phases. Pure `ProcessBuilder` + `java.nio`, so *no embedded jar*
  (simpler than dbus, which embedded three for a transport `ServiceLoader`).
* *cluster contribution* — a *fragment of `doctor-core`* carrying both faces: the *mediator*
  `@Component` (composes the cluster-edge contact with the systemd node-fact read through `systemd-contract`,
  and produces the `Observation` — time 1) and `ClusterSpecialist` (reasons over it — time 2, moved out
  of `doctor-core` into the fragment). No `cluster-core`: the contribution *is* the cluster's logic.

What does *not* move makes the cut legible. The executable (`seed-outcluster`) keeps only *time* — the
`SeedNodeBootstrapWatcher` retry/timeout poll, preview/dry-run, the Pulumi-resource wrapping, the
runbook renderer. The kubeconfig file-poll (`java.nio`, a local file) and the systemd phase-0 gate stay
host too: they orchestrate, they do not *contact* the cluster. Only the kubectl contact is the edge.
And the coupling cluster↔systemd lives in the fragment's `Import-Package`, so `doctor-core`'s manifest
still names no domain.

*Scope — cluster-first.* cluster is the *first* contributed specialist; `DbusTcpSpecialist` and
`NetworkSpecialist` stay hard-wired in `doctor-core` for now, migrated in a later increment — the same
"prove the pattern on one instance first" as pulumi-edge being the first edge. The roster is
transitionally mixed; that is accepted, not a half-migration left to rot.

== The two principles this settles

. *Synthesis takes prerequisites; it does not fetch them.* A describe-step is a pure function of its
  inputs. Reaching out for an input mid-describe is the defect — independent of which world does the
  reaching.
. *The edge materialises the boundary even when the domain could do the contact inline.* An edge is not
  a playability concession; it is the system's skin made explicit. Playability decides only *which world*
  the edge lives in, never *whether* it is one.

== Quick decision guide

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| You have… | Then…

| Logic that reasons over the model, no external contact
| `-core` (domain). Owns the ports it calls.

| The contract a consumer calls outward
| `-contract` (an installed `type=contract` bundle), owned by that consumer. Pure. Named by concern,
  never `…In/…Out`.

| An adapter that contacts one external target
| `<target>-edge`. Implements a consumer's port. Owns NO port, has NO `-core` unless it also carries real
  domain logic (it usually doesn't).

| A pure point of contact with no reasoning (ssh-to-age)
| Just the `<target>-edge` + a port owned by its consumer. No `-core`, no port of its own.

| A domain whose meaning *presupposes another domain's fact* (cluster readiness presupposes systemd)
| A *contribution* — a fragment of the host that fans the reasoning out (the doctor). It carries the
  mediator (produces the fact, reaching peers through their ports) and the reasoner. No `-core`; the
  coupling lives in the fragment, the host stays blind. See <<the-contribution-role>>.
|===
