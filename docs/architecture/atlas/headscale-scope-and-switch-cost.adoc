= Headscale — Scope and the Cost of Switching
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 3

toc::[]

**Status:** 📋 decision input, 2026-10-07. **Nothing here is a plan to execute.**

== Why this document exists

The operator asked for the *scope of headscale* and *the consequences of switching* — explicitly not
for a switch. So this answers two questions and stops:

. what headscale gives us, and what it does **not**;
. what moving the **clusters** onto it would actually cost, as opposed to what it looks like it
  would cost.

The infrastructure side is already decided and specified elsewhere — see
xref:fabric-control-plane.adoc[the fabric control-plane migration] and ndh's
`docs/fabric-control-plane-spec.adoc`. This document is about the part that is **not** decided:
step 12, the clusters.

== What headscale is, for us

A self-hosted Tailscale **control plane**: it registers nodes, distributes policy, and answers the
coordination traffic that `tailscaled` would otherwise send to Tailscale's SaaS. The **data plane is
unchanged** — it is still WireGuard between the same clients, because we run the same `tailscaled`.

....

That boundary is the whole reason a switch is even thinkable: we are not replacing the network, only
who tells it what it may do.

=== The scope — what we get

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Capability | What it gives us

| **Node registration** | pre-auth keys and a device list we own, so a machine joins without a
vendor account
| **Policy: `grants` + `ssh` rules** | the access model the fabric rests on — access becomes a grant
instead of a key. ⚠️ `grants` arrive in **0.29.0**; this is the reason for the version floor
| **Split DNS** | `dns.nameservers.split` points a domain at a chosen resolver, which is how a
member resolves the fabric zone served by its segment's dnsmasq
| **Subnet routes** | a member advertises a CIDR and others accept it — how the `vmnet` and fabric
spans are reached
| **Tailscale SSH** | keyless SSH mediated by policy. ⚠️ Our fork intercepts it on port 2222; the
port is ours, not upstream's
|===

=== ❌ The scope — what we do NOT get, and must not assume

Each of these was measured against the upstream project, not inferred:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Absent | Evidence, and the consequence

| **Funnel** | a **SaaS** feature: public ingress through Tailscale's own edge. headscale has no
equivalent and cannot have one — it does not operate an edge. ⛔ *This is the single biggest cost of
a cluster switch; see below.*
| **Tailscale Services** | no equivalent. Our screen access therefore uses **subnet routes + grants**
instead of `svc:` targets
| **Federation** | `#1973`, closed **not-planned**. Two control planes do not know about each other —
hence the WireGuard bridge between the gateways
| **HA** | `#2695`; the maintainer's answer is *"The short answer is no"*. One control plane per
bare-metal is a consequence, not a preference
| **`tailscale cert`** | `#2137`, open. headscale issues no certificates, so the control URL's TLS
leaf is ours to mint and distribute
|===

IMPORTANT: Four of these five absences are already **designed around** in the infrastructure plan.
Only the Funnel has no design around it, because only the clusters use it.

== The version floor is now a deadline, not a defect

`modules/.common.d/headscale-pkg.nix` pins the band `>= 0.29, < 0.30`. The ceiling was written as a
tripwire — *we deliberately do not need `/api/v2` + OAuth while the clusters stay on the SaaS.*

⚠️ That reasoning is correct **today and only today**. `0.30` is precisely the release that makes the
Kubernetes operator able to drive headscale, so the ceiling is the thing gating step 12. It stops
being a guard rail and becomes an **expiry date**.

== The cost of switching the clusters

=== ✅ What is already in place — the part that genuinely is a flag

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Piece | State

| The operator honours a custom login server
| `cmd/k8s-operator/operator.go` on upstream `main` reads `OPERATOR_LOGIN_SERVER` and propagates it
to the resources it manages. The historical blocker — *"the operator does not pass a custom login
server to the proxy containers"* — **no longer exists**
| The chart exposes it
| a top-level `loginServer`, from chart **1.98.4**. We pin `Component.TAILSCALE = "1.102.3"`, so **we
already have it**
| We already feed the operator an OAuth client
| `TailscaleManifestsUnit` deploys the chart, and the `operator-oauth` Secret is served by
`TailscaleOauthClientGateway` from a `tskey-client-…` that **ndh** mints and mirrors — today against
the SaaS
|===

⚠️ **Do not look for that capability through the PRs the upstream issue cites.**
`tailscale/tailscale#11627` (*"add basic support for Headscale"*) and `#10223` (*"base URL env var"*)
are **both closed without merge**. Searching by them finds nothing and reads as "unsupported"; the
capability arrived another way.

So the mechanical delta is small: a `loginServer` in `valuesContent`, and a gateway pointed at a
client minted by `headscale oauth-clients` with scopes and tags.

=== ✅ Coexistence is a supported feature, not a workaround — the `Tailnet` CRD

⛔ *An earlier revision of this document framed the switch around `OPERATOR_LOGIN_SERVER` and then
offered "keep the funnel path on the SaaS" as one of three uncomfortable options. Both were wrong:*
`OPERATOR_LOGIN_SERVER` moves **everything**, Funnel included, and keeping two tailnets side by side
is not a split hack — **the operator supports it natively**.

*Measured on the exact chart version we pin (`1.102.3`; present since `1.98.10`):*

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Mechanism | Shape

| `tailnets.tailscale.com`
| a **cluster-scoped** CRD: `spec.loginUrl` plus `spec.credentials.secretName` (`client_id` +
`client_secret`, or `audience` for workload identity)
| `Connector.spec.tailnet`, `ProxyGroup.spec.tailnet`
| empty ⇒ the **default** tailnet (the chart's own); otherwise the name of a `Tailnet` CR. So the
choice is **per resource**, opt-in
| the reconciler
| `reconciler/tailnet` is registered in `operator.go` with **no feature gate**, with an API-client
factory `For(tailnet string)`
|===

So the default stays the SaaS — **the Funnel and its annotations keep working, untouched** — and one
`Tailnet` CR per headscale control plane is added beside it. That dovetails with
`netplan.tailnets = { saas, bioskop, nikopol }`: the CR names would come from that catalogue rather
than being invented.

⚠️ **`spec.tailnet` is immutable** (CEL `self == oldSelf`). Moving a resource from one tailnet to the
other therefore means **delete and recreate**, which means a **new device identity** — so this is a
placement decision taken once per resource, not a dial.

⬜ **Not verified, and load-bearing enough to say so:** whether proxies created by *annotation*
(Ingress/Service) and the operator's **own** device can leave the default tailnet — they very
probably cannot. And upstream's own harness drives `loginServer`, **not** `Tailnet`, so the
`Tailnet` → headscale path is **untested upstream**. It still needs headscale **0.30** regardless,
because the operator mints each proxy's auth key through `/api/v2` + OAuth.

=== ⛔ What remains an arbitration rather than a port

**The Funnel is SaaS-only, and we use it.** *Measured in this repo:* `Funnel`, `FunnelLeaf`,
`PacWebhookFunnel` and `FunnelCertIssuance` are ingress-domain types, and `TailscaleManifestsUnit`
imports `FunnelCertIssuance` with an apparatus of cert restore and state persistence around it —
`FunnelCertRestoreManifestsUnit`, `FunnelStatePersistenceManifestsUnit`.

ⓘ With the `Tailnet` CRD the arbitration **shrinks but does not vanish**: the funnel path staying on
the vendor is now a supported placement rather than a concession, so the live question is no longer
*"what replaces the Funnel"* but *"which resources are worth moving at all"* — and that is still a
design call, not a release.

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Option | What it means

| **Keep the funnel path on the SaaS**
| ✅ Consistent with a decision already taken: *the SaaS is not a participant in the carve, it is a
vendor given.* The clusters would then be **split** — funnel ingress on the vendor's control plane,
everything else on ours. Two `tailscaled` identities per cluster, or a boundary drawn at the
workload
| **Replace it**
| what replaces public ingress-through-a-vendor-edge is not a headscale feature but an
**architecture**: our own edge, our own certificates, our own DNS. That is a chantier, not a switch
| **Give it up**
| the honest third option, and it has to be stated: whatever the funnel serves publicly stops being
served publicly
|===

IMPORTANT: This is the decision the switch actually requires, and it is **not technical**. No
upstream release unblocks it.

=== ⛔ Two hard constraints, read in headscale's own test harness

Both come from `integration/k3sic/` — upstream's own notes on making this work, which is a stronger
source than a feature list.

**A private CA does not work for pods.** `tailscaled` verifies against the **system** trust store,
and there is no way to project a CA file into a proxy pod:

* `ProxyClass.spec.statefulSet.pod` has no `volumes`;
* `tailscaleContainer` has no `volumeMounts`;
* its `env` is a reduced schema — `name`/`value`, no `valueFrom`;
* `operatorConfig` has `extraEnv` but no `extraVolumes`;
* `SSL_CERT_FILE` *is* honoured — and wants a file you cannot mount.

Upstream's conclusion is to **bake the CA into derived images** (operator + proxy), import them into
containerd, and force `imagePullPolicy: Never` through a `ProxyClass`. In practice **they gave up and
serve headscale over plain HTTP** in the test.

[IMPORTANT]
====
*Scope — this does not touch the infrastructure plan.* Steps 2–6 of
xref:fabric-control-plane.adoc[the migration] put headscale's members on **our machines**, where we
own the system trust store (`security.pki.certificates` on NixOS, the System keychain on darwin).
That is exactly why a leaf from the private `mammoth-skate-tls` root works there.

The constraint bites **only when pods become members** — step 12. It is a design input for *that*
step, not a reason to revisit the container's TLS today. The Incus container that *runs* headscale is
a **server**, not a client, so it is not concerned at all.

⬜ *One reserve, stated as an unmeasured hypothesis rather than a finding:* a `tailscaled` launched
from a **flox/nix** environment may inherit `SSL_CERT_FILE` pointing at nix's own `cacert` bundle,
which would **bypass the system trust store even on a NixOS host** — and then the constraint bites
earlier than step 12. One line settles it per member:

[source,bash]
----
tr '\0' '\n' < /proc/$(pgrep -x tailscaled)/environ | grep SSL_CERT
----

⚠️ And a second URL the private leaf has to cover: the **embedded DERP presents its own
certificate**, verified by the same trust store. Same rule, one more name.
====

**DERP, or no data plane.** A pod's only endpoint is an unroutable pod IP, so its sole path to an
off-cluster node is **DERP**. A proxy composes DERP over HTTPS, so a DERP without usable TLS relays
nothing. Upstream injects `TS_DEBUG_DERP_WS_CLIENT` + `TS_DEBUG_USE_DERP_HTTP` through a
`ProxyClass`. Without that, ingress and egress proxies have **no data plane at all** — not degraded,
absent.

ⓘ A smaller trap worth keeping: the chart interpolates `oauth.clientId` / `clientSecret`
**unquoted**, so an all-digits identifier becomes a YAML number and the apiserver rejects it.
Upstream creates the Secret with `--from-literal`. We create the Secret ourselves, so this costs
nothing — provided the values stay strings.

=== ★ Coexistence changes the status of the CGNAT carve — and the carve is already there

Both the SaaS and headscale draw addresses from `100.64.0.0/10` by default. A proxy pod belongs to
**one** tailnet, so there is no collision *on a pod*. But anything reasoning about `100.x` at
**cluster scale** — egress `ExternalName`, network policies, the doctor — becomes ambiguous the day
two tailnets are present in one cluster.

✅ *The addressing work is already done.* ndh's catalogue carves our two tailnets out of the range,
measured on the chantier branch:

[cols="1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| tailnet | `allocation` | `prefixes.v4`

| `saas` | `vendor` | `100.64.0.0/10` — an opaque **given**, we do not control its allocator
| `bioskop` | `ours` | `100.127.0.0/16`
| `nikopol` | `ours` | `100.126.0.0/16`
|===

⛔ **But this retires a caveat I wrote, and the retirement is the point of this section.** The carve
is documented — in the spec, in the migration atlas, and in its own code comment — as being *for
legibility only, with nothing permitted to depend on it*. The stated reason: **no machine is ever on
two tailnets**, so a collision is never resolved on a host.

That premise still holds **per host**. What coexistence introduces is a **cluster-scope consumer**:
one cluster with pods on two tailnets, where telling the ranges apart stops being a convenience and
becomes a correctness condition for anything matching on `100.x`.

[IMPORTANT]
====
So the caveat needs a condition rather than a deletion: *the carve is legibility-only **until** two
tailnets coexist inside one cluster; from that point something does depend on it.*

ⓘ The pleasant part: nothing new has to be allocated. A carve written for legibility turns out to be
the **enabling condition** for coexistence — which is the opposite of the usual outcome, where a
decision taken "just for readability" has to be undone.
====

== Verdict

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Question | Answer

| Is it technically blocked upstream?
| **No longer.** The operator capability is merged, tested in upstream CI, and the chart version we
already pin exposes it
| Is it usable today?
| ❌ **No.** `0.30` is unreleased, the milestone has 25 open issues and no date, and the ndh version
band excludes `0.30` by construction — so even release day needs a band re-aim, not a bump
| Is the remaining work small?
| The **code** delta is small. The **decision** is not: the Funnel has no headscale equivalent, and
choosing among keep-on-SaaS / replace / give-up is an architecture call
| What should change today?
| Nothing executable. Three things become *known*: the target stops being speculative and becomes a
calendar question; the version ceiling becomes an expiry date; and the private-CA constraint becomes
a design input for step 12 — to be answered **before** that step is planned, not during it
|===
